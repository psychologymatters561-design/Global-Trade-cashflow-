What we found

The deficit fell. Almost nobody meant it to.

Three numbers carry the year. Each one contradicts the story people were telling in 2025.

US trade gap · Jan–Jul 2026
−29.6%

The goods and services gap fell by $188.4bn against the same months of 2025. Exports rose 12.0 percent while imports moved just 1.9 percent.

BEA, released 3 Sep 2026

Brent crude · 6 Sep 2026
$96.82

Up about 47 percent on the year. The Strait of Hormuz closed in late February and Gulf output was cut by at least 10 million barrels a day.

IEA and World Bank commodity data

China exports · Jan–Jul 2026
+18.5%

Semiconductor export value nearly doubled. ASEAN overtook America as the top destination, at 18 percent of exports against 11.

China General Administration of Customs

Put together, they describe something other than a policy win. The tariffs that were supposed to close the American deficit were voided by the Supreme Court on 20 February 2026, six votes to three, on the ground that emergency powers never granted the tariff authority in the first place. What actually narrowed the gap was a war: with the Gulf shut in, the United States sold energy into a panicked market, and exports did the work that duties could not.

China, meanwhile, did not stop exporting. It changed address. Roughly $77bn of American-bound goods were rerouted through Southeast Asia from April onward, and the export mix moved up the value chain into chips and AI hardware even as shipments to the United States fell.

Method and limits

How to read this, and where it stops

Research you cannot check is marketing. Here is what we did, and what we deliberately did not do.

What is in here

Official releases only, each one dated. United States trade through July 2026 from the BEA release of 3 September. China trade through July 2026 from the General Administration of Customs. India through July 2026 from the Ministry of Commerce, with GDP from MoSPI and reserves from the RBI. Reserve currency shares from IMF COFER, Q1 2026.

What is not

No forecasts dressed as data. No annualised part-years. The bar charts stop at 2025 because that is the last complete year; 2026 appears only as year to date, labelled as such. Where a number is an estimate, it says so next to the number rather than in a footnote.

Honest caveats

Sector tables are 2025 composition, not 2026
Commodity-level detail is not published mid-year at this granularity. Rather than fill 90 line items with guesses, the tables keep complete 2025 full-year values and say so in the heading. Where 2026 movement is verified for a specific line, it is named on that line.
Partner shares are 2025; 2026 movement sits in the notes
Mixing 2026 year-to-date values into a 2025 share table would produce percentages that do not sum and rankings that mislead. The quantitative columns stay on one consistent basis and each note carries the verified 2026 change.
India's GDP ranking is contested in public commentary
India is the world's sixth largest economy at $4.15tn on the IMF's April 2026 count, behind Japan at $4.38tn and the United Kingdom at $4.27tn. Widely repeated claims that it holds fourth place date from 2025 estimates made before an 11 percent rupee depreciation and the MoSPI base-year revision. Neither is a growth slowdown.
Figures get revised
Trade statistics are routinely restated for months after first publication. Every figure here carries its release date so you can check it against the current print rather than trusting this page.
